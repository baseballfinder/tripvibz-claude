How we verify locals

Last updated November 17, 2026

A local badge only means something if it's hard to fake. This page explains what each badge means and how the ✓ is earned. We explain how it works but leave out the exact numbers, because those would mostly help people trying to cheat it.

What the badges mean

  • Local · 10+ yrs ✓

    Verified local. We've seen this person sign in from near the city on several different days, spread over several weeks. The years are what they told us about how long they've lived there.

  • Local · 10+ yrs · unverified

    Says they're local, not verified yet. They signed in and told us they live there, and we haven't seen them near the city enough times yet. Every new local starts here. Anyone who only visits the site from far away stays here.

  • Unverified

    Anonymous, or badge removed. Posted without signing in, or an editor took away this person's local badge for the city (more on that below).

  • Founding local

    One of the first ten verified locals to post in a city. You need the ✓ to get it.

  • Regular visitor

    Not claiming to be local. They told us they visit often. Their takes count like anyone else's.

How the ✓ is earned

  • Where your connection says you are. Every internet connection has a rough location, usually the city your provider routes you through. When you're signed in and open TripVibz or post a take, we check whether that location is within about 40 miles of each city on the site. You won't see a prompt, and we never ask your browser for GPS.
  • It takes weeks. One check only shows you were near the city that day, which is just as true of a tourist. For the ✓ we need to see you near the city on several separate days spread over several weeks. A long weekend won't do it, and neither will a two-week vacation. If you live there and use the site normally, expect it in about a month.
  • One city at a time. Someone verified in Miami is still "unverified" in Denver until they earn it there.
  • Hosting and VPN networks don't count. A connection from a data center, cloud service or VPN tells us nothing about where you live. We keep a note of it for our editors, and it never counts toward the ✓.
  • You can't buy it or ask for it. There's no form. The years on a badge are what the person told us. The ✓ is the only part we checked ourselves.
  • Editors can remove it. If someone with a local badge posts spam or ads, or clearly isn't from there, an editor removes the badge for that city. It doesn't come back by itself, and that person's takes show as Unverified from then on.

What it doesn't prove

Being near a city again and again over several weeks is a good sign that someone lives there. It isn't proof. A daily commuter or a student in town for a semester can earn the ✓ honestly, and they do know the place. A resident whose phone network routes through another metro may wait longer for it, or get it for the neighboring city by mistake. So the badge says "Local" instead of "Resident", and everyone's votes decide which takes move up. If a badge looks wrong to you, tell us.

What we store

For each signed-in person and each city we keep four things: whether they were near it at the last check, how many separate days we've seen them near it, the first and last of those dates, and whether an editor removed the badge. We don't keep your IP address, your coordinates or a history of where you've been. The privacy page has the full detail and explains how to have it deleted.

Why we built it this way

Asking people to prove where they live doesn't work, because almost nobody will do that to post a one-sentence tip. Trusting whatever people type doesn't work either. Checking where you connect from over a few weeks costs a real local nothing and takes real effort to fake. It will get some people wrong, and we'll adjust it as the site grows. If you think it's wrong about someone, email hello@tripvibz.com.
