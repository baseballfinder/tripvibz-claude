About TripVibz

Ask someone who knows.

Review sites tell you where to go based on people who were in town for a weekend. TripVibz asks the people who know a city: where they'd take a friend, what to skip and when to come.

How it works

  • Questions. Every city has a short set of questions locals argue about, like "Worth it, or skip it?" or "Best time to visit?". Answering is one tap. Don't see the question you'd ask? Suggest it and we'll add it.
  • Takes. Add a sentence to an answer and it becomes your take: where you'd take a friend, what to skip, when to go. Everyone votes True or Nope, so the best advice rises.
  • Where they'd take a friend to eat. Each signed-in person names their top 3 restaurants, with a line on why. The city page rolls them up, verified locals first.
  • The Guide. Each city's map of the spots people's takes are about and the top restaurant picks, with how many people picked each place and what they said, then where to stay. When you add a take, say where it is and it goes on the map.
  • Vibes. Tag a "Do this" take or a restaurant with who it's for and what kind of time it is, like Date night, With the kids or Loud and late. A vibe shows once two different people give it, and a city gets a page for a vibe (like Date night in Chicago) once people have tagged places with it.
  • Local badges. Signed-in people show how long they've lived in a city. The ✓ is earned by being seen near the city on several days over several weeks, not by anything they type; until then the badge says "unverified". How we verify locals.
  • The month-by-month calendar. Festivals, conventions, weather and price seasons are researched by the TripVibz editors from official sources and checked monthly. Each one links to its source.

Who's behind it

TripVibz is built by James Overholt, who also publishes Miami Take, a South Florida travel site. Miami Take's most-read stories were always the things a friend who lives there would tell you, so TripVibz asks the people who know every city.
