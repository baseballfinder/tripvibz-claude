About TripVibz

Ask someone who knows.

Most review sites are written by people who were in town for a weekend. TripVibz asks the people who live there instead: where would you take a friend, and what would you tell them to skip?

How it works

  • Questions. Every city has a handful of questions people argue about, like "Navy Pier: worth it, or skip it?" One tap answers it. Don't see the one you'd ask? Suggest it and I'll take a look.
  • Takes. Add a sentence to your answer and it becomes a take. Other people mark it True or Nope, and the ones most people agree with move up.
  • Where they'd take a friend to eat. Sign in and name your top 3 restaurants in a city, with a line on why. The city page adds everyone's picks together and puts verified locals' picks first.
  • The Guide. A map of each city showing the spots people's takes are about and the restaurants they picked, with how many people picked each one and what they said. Well-rated hotels near those spots are at the bottom, with a price, the guest rating and how far each is from the places people recommend. When you add a take, say where it is and it lands on the map.
  • Vibes. Tag a "Do this" take or a restaurant with who it's for or what kind of night it is, like Date night, With the kids or Loud and late. A vibe shows up once two people have given it. When people have tagged places in a city with the same vibe, it gets its own page, like Date night in Chicago.
  • Local badges. If you sign in, you can say how long you've lived in a city. The ✓ comes later, after we've seen you near the city on several days over several weeks. Until then your badge says "unverified". How we verify locals.
  • The month-by-month calendar. Festivals, conventions, the weather, and the months when hotels get expensive. Every entry links to the official source it came from, and they're rechecked every month.

Who's behind it

I'm James Overholt. I also publish Miami Take, a South Florida travel site. The stories people read most there were always the things a friend who lives in Miami would tell you, so I built TripVibz to ask that friend in every city.

TripVibz pays for itself with hotel links. If you book through one, the booking site pays me a commission.
